About

Colometrics is a study tool for the Tanakh in the original language. It displays the biblical text with the Masoretic cantillation accents (teʿamim) and divides each verse into cola — prosodic lines — according to the classical Tiberian accent hierarchy of Wickes (1881, 1887) and Park (2020).

Colometric Analyzer

Choose a book, chapter and verse, then pick a colometric view. The teʿamim are a hierarchical system: the disjunctive accents (mafsikim) close prosodic units of different ranks. The Poetry and Prose views apply the accent hierarchy of the poetic books (Psalms, Job, Proverbs) or of the twenty-one prose books respectively, so any verse can be examined under either system. Click a word to see the name and role of its accent sign(s); where a sign only exists in combination — legarmeh (an accent + paseq), reviʿa mugrash (geresh muqdam + revia), ʿole we-yored — the compound is identified.

Reader

Read any chapter in four forms: fully accented (Miqra according to the Masorah), unaccented (vowel points only), consonantal (letters only), or scholarly transliteration, with an optional English translation (JPS) in parallel.

Word study

Every word is segmented into its morphemes (conjunctions, prepositions, the article, pronominal suffixes) and each morpheme carries its parsing — stem, conjugation, person, gender, number, state — from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible morphology. Click a morpheme for a quick lexicon lookup of that part. The word's Brown–Driver–Briggs entry (via Sefaria's “BDB Augmented Strong” lexicon) appears in a compact abridged form; a button reveals the full unabridged BDB article with all its scriptural citations.

Technical notes

The application is a small Python server (standard library only) with a static frontend; Sefaria API responses are cached on disk in sefaria_cache/. U+05BD is read as silluq on the verse-final word and as meteg (gaʿya) elsewhere. In the poetic books the character named ZARQA in Unicode (U+0598) is the conjunctive tsinnorit and ZINOR (U+05AE) the disjunctive, per Unicode Technical Note #27.
